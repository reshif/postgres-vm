# ADR-0015: Curation Capacity and the Initial Curator

## Context

The two-plane model and the trust lattice both depend on a human reviewing quarantined candidates, resolving conflicts, and approving promotions into authoritative knowledge. Curation scaling is identified in the blueprint's risk register as high-likelihood and severe: the common failure is not bad code, it is that nobody triages the inbox in week nine.

No dedicated curator headcount exists, and inventing one would be pretending curation is free.

## Decision

**The initial curator is the owner and primary maintainer of the memory-platform repository.** Curation is a lightweight engineering responsibility attached to project ownership, not a new role.

The role is bounded, and the bounds are the point:

- **Capacity cap: 30 minutes per week.** Not a target — a ceiling.
- **Scope: high-signal items only** — flagged candidates, unresolved conflicts, and proposed Plane A promotions. Not everything the system observes.
- **Plane A review happens in git**, through ordinary code review. Only Plane B candidates go through the console inbox. The two review paths do not mix.
- **Target trajectory:** under 10 minutes per week per project before expanding beyond three projects.
- **Operational targets:** inbox depth under 40 per project; alert at 100.
- **Kill switch:** if inbox depth exceeds 200 for two weeks with no reviewer, auto-extraction is disabled automatically, not by discussion.
- **Expansion gate:** if the 30-minute cap is exceeded four consecutive weeks, Phase 5+ expansion pauses until extraction quality or review automation improves.

The 30-minute cap is treated as a **falsifiable architectural claim**, not an aspiration. If curation requires a dedicated human, the architecture has not demonstrated that curation scales, and that is a finding about the design rather than a staffing request.

## Fallback if no curator exists

If at any point no curator is available, the system degrades rather than continuing unsupervised:

- LLM extraction is disabled entirely.
- Extraction becomes deterministic only — classifier and rule based, capped at trust tier `observed`, never authoritative.
- Derived memories decay aggressively (30-day half-life) and rank below all git-backed knowledge.
- Reflection is disabled.
- Cross-project generalisation stays off (it already is, per ADR-0012).

Plane A remains fully useful in this mode. A git-backed knowledge base plus a searchable deterministic episode store is a smaller product that works with no human in the loop, and it is strictly better than an unreviewed quarantine pipeline. Auto-extraction without review is a security failure mode, not a convenience.

## Alternatives considered

1. Assign a named engineer other than the owner — rejected: no such person is identified, and inventing one would encode a fact we do not have.
2. Create a dedicated curator role — rejected: unjustified headcount, and it hides the scaling question the cap is designed to expose.
3. Automate promotion — rejected outright: promotion is a security boundary (ADR-0005).
4. Proceed with no curator and keep LLM extraction — rejected: this is the documented path to an unreviewed, poisonable corpus.

## Consequences

Curation cost is explicit and measured rather than assumed away.

Extraction conservatism is now load-bearing: an extractor with an acceptance rate below 30% is generating noise and must be tightened, not compensated for by auto-promotion.

Expansion beyond a handful of projects is gated on demonstrated review efficiency, which is the correct constraint.

## Acceptance test

Over four consecutive weeks, the curator keeps inbox depth under 40 per project within the 30-minute weekly cap, and the extraction acceptance rate stays inside the 30–85% band.
